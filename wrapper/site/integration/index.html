Basel can map heat and heavy rain. Deciding what to change on a street is the hard part.

Heat and rain information exists, but it is read in separate places. SpongeSquad connects it into one path. Find a place worth investigating and see what is known about it. Then test one sponge-city change on an example street, and name what must be established before anyone can decide.

Published

The signals exist, separately

Basel publishes an urban climate analysis, a tree cadastre with daily updates, LiDAR canopy cover and official land cover. Federal overland-flow hazard maps are also open. They answer different questions in different places.

Derived · not validated

Most sealed ground is not street

The team overlaid open land cover and parcels. Of about 1,650 ha of sealed ground in Basel-Stadt, about 18 % lies on public street space (Allmend) and 82 % on other parcels. This is a screening figure computed by the team, not an official statistic.

Gated or unmeasured

The deciding facts are hard to reach

The sewer network, gully points and utility depths were not found as open data. Local infiltration capacity has to be tested on site. Without them, no street design can move beyond an illustration.
