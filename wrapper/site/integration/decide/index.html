3 · Investigations and approvals

What must be established before a proposal advances

Only rules found in Basel sources are stated as rules. Guidance from Bern, Zürich and Luzern is useful background but is not a Basel requirement.

Established for Basel

Rules and practice found

  • Green roofs. § 72 BPG: flat roofs of 10 m² or more must be greened when built or renovated, with at least 15 cm of substrate. Source
  • Rainwater fee. CHF 0.90 per m² of sealed area connected to the sewer. Source
  • Works coordination. The canton uses the district-heating build-out to redesign streets with unsealing and greening. Source
  • Pilots under monitoring. Sponge-city elements in the new VoltaNord streets, with five years of scientific monitoring. Source

Not yet established

  • Basel-specific planning and approval guidance for unsealing, infiltration, tree pits, retention and green roofs

  • Who must sign off a rain garden that takes road water in a Basel street

Investigations for a candidate site

Steps the team's gatekeeper model proposes before any footprint is drawn. They are a working checklist, not a documented Basel procedure.

  1. Fix a footprint and check its parcels and public-realm status.
  2. Check groundwater protection zones (data.bs.ch 100292) and nearby groundwater statistics.
  3. Request an area-limited utility extract and confirm with the network operator before any excavation.
  4. Establish where retained and overflow water can go with the drainage owners.
  5. Test local infiltration on site.
  6. Check permitted works nearby for a chance to dig once.

How people could take part

Existing routes are separated from the team's ideas. Formal participation in street projects has not been researched yet.

Advice and grants

The canton advises owners and offers grants from the value-added levy fund and Swisslos for citizen initiatives. Source

Announced

Private greening fund

A funding programme for unsealing, trees and green space on private property, planned for 2026 if the Grand Council approves it. Source

Open question

Shaping a street project

How residents can comment on or propose a change to a specific Basel street is not yet researched. This page does not claim a procedure.

Team prototype

Rain Walk: residents as evidence collectors

A demo campaign in which people record what they see on a street, such as paving, gullies or tree pits, and review each other's notes. It is a prototype on a synthetic segment, not an official participation channel. A visual observation never establishes what is underground. Open Rain Walk
