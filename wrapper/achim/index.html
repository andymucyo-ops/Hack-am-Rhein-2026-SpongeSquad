The proposition

The map finds a signal. The system finds out what that signal permits us to do.

AI and computation can connect evidence, expose gaps and illustrate possibilities. They cannot turn missing knowledge into a favourable fact.

Upstream contract

A candidate arrives with identity—not invented street geometry.

Andy’s scoping tool can nominate a promising area and pass a versioned candidate context. The handoff carries the place name, coordinates, sources, constraints and evidence questions. It does not turn a district score into soil, utilities or drainage facts.

Evidence survives the journey

The example never collapses into one score

At every stage, the user can still distinguish evidence from design fiction.

Known or sourced

  • Candidate identity and coordinates
  • Bundled city-level context and source references
  • Basel rain-garden precedent

Illustrative or assumed

  • The demonstration street geometry
  • The candidate strip and rain-garden design
  • The Street Lab storm and performance parameters

Still blocking a real decision

  • Underground utilities and ownership
  • Soil infiltration and groundwater clearance
  • Actual drainage connections and engineering feasibility

The boundary

What this system does—and does not do

Does

Make a mechanism understandable

It shows why surfaces, storage, soil, drainage and overflow must work as a connected system.

Does

Turn unknowns into a next action

It identifies what must be requested, observed, tested or decided—and who may hold that evidence.

Does not

Issue an engineering recommendation

A map signal or interactive scenario never proves that an intervention is feasible at a real site.
